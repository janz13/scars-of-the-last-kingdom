## 1. Core Premise

The player wakes in the underground metro tunnels of a dead city. A violet sigil burns on their left wrist — it wasn't there before. It pulses like a second heartbeat. It reacts to things that should be inert. The city above is rubble and patrols. The city below is factions, scarcity, and secrets. Five years have passed since the Blackout killed four hundred thousand people in a week.

The game is not about surviving. Survival is the baseline. The game is about **what the player becomes** in the space between the ruin and the answer — and whether, when the answer arrives, they are ready to carry it.

The player's mark is the key to the central mystery: the collapse of civilisation was not an accident, and the truth of what caused it has been waiting, underground, for someone with exactly the right signal to come close enough to hear it.

## 2. World History

### The Age of Extraction (100–50 years before the game)
Industrial overreach destabilises the region. Aquifers collapse. Agricultural zones fail. The government responds with aggressive urban consolidation, moving displaced populations into high-density metro zones. The underground metro is expanded far beyond its original design. Deep excavation teams find structures that predate any recorded civilisation. The government classifies the findings. The Last Archive is founded in secret to study them.

### The Compact Period (50–20 years before the game)
Surface resources are rationed. A political coalition called the Glass Court rises to power, composed of scientists, engineers, and former officials who believe that relic technology can stabilise the collapse. They succeed, partially. The city stays alive longer than it should. The Iron Saints are established to enforce rationing mandates. Underground resistance multiplies. The Red Choir begins broadcasting.

### The Unravelling (20–5 years before the game)
Relic technology, incompletely understood, is integrated into the city's power infrastructure. The Last Archive warns that the integration is unstable. The Glass Court suppresses the warning. For seventeen years the city runs on a grid partially sustained by relic energy that nobody fully understands. Deep in the metro excavations, archaeologists find the Vault — a sealed chamber of relic density unlike anything previously discovered. The Last Archive maps it. The Glass Court wants to open it. The Iron Saints are dispatched to secure the site. The Red Choir says it is a tomb.

### The Blackout (5 years before the game)
The Vault is opened by a Glass Court extraction team under Director Kael's authorisation. Seventeen seconds later, every power system in the city — conventional and relic — fails simultaneously. Four hundred thousand people die in the first week from failed infrastructure: medical systems, heating, water treatment, food refrigeration. The survivors call this The Blackout. Nobody agrees on why it happened. Nobody has gone back to the Vault.

### The Present (game start)
Five years after the Blackout. Most surviving population lives underground in the metro network. Factions have carved out territory. Resources are critical. Relic objects still exist and still respond to contact. Nobody understands why. The player wakes with a mark that didn't exist yesterday.

## 3. The Blackout

The Blackout was not a conventional power failure. It was not sabotage, electromagnetic pulse, or infrastructure decay. **Something responded** to the opening of the Vault. Every system in the city ceased operation within seventeen seconds of the Vault breach. The failure had a boundary, roughly following the old city limits. Outside that boundary, the world continued as normal.

### Immediate Consequences
- Hospitals lose power. Patients on life support die within hours.
- Metro system locks in place, trapping thousands underground.
- Water treatment stops. Contamination spreads within days.
- Food refrigeration fails. Stored provisions rot within a week.
- Communication severs. Nobody can call for help.

### Long-Term Consequences
- Population drops from approximately 2.4 million to an estimated 80,000 survivors in five years.
- The metro becomes the primary habitation zone for survivors.
- Relic objects become the most valuable trade commodity.
- Pre-Blackout social structures collapse. New factions form around resource control.
- A generation of children grows up in the metro who have never seen the surface in daylight.

### What Each Faction Calls It

| Faction | Their Name | What It Means to Them |
|---|---|---|
| Iron Saints | Day Zero | The day order died and had to be rebuilt |
| Glass Court | The Incident | A technical failure to be corrected through better data |
| Hollow Children | The Collapse | The night the city above stopped protecting the city below |
| Red Choir | The Opening | The Vault completing its purpose |
| Last Archive | The Response | Something answering the Vault opening |

### What Nobody Knows
Nobody knows what is in the Vault. Nobody knows why the wrist marks appeared after the Blackout. The Iron Saints scan for marks at checkpoints. They do not explain why. The extraction team that opened the Vault disappeared — their equipment was found neatly arranged outside the entrance. None of their bodies were ever recovered.

## 4. Relic Technology

Relics are objects — sometimes tools, sometimes structures, sometimes indistinguishable from natural stone — that predate recorded civilisation by an unknown period. They were first found in deep excavations during the Age of Extraction. They are not made of known materials. They do not follow conventional physics when powered. They were clearly manufactured by intelligence, but by whom, and for what purpose, is unknown.

### How They Work
Relic technology responds to biological contact — specifically to certain bioelectric signatures. Most humans produce no response. A small number of marked individuals produce a response that can power relic objects, open relic locks, and in some cases trigger relic displays of information in an unreadable language.

### Types of Relics

| Type | Description | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Relic Shards** | Small fragments, usually palm-sized. Some emit heat or light. Some do nothing until touched by a mark. | High — one shard buys a week of clean water |
| **Relic Locks** | Sealed doors or containers that cannot be forced. Respond only to mark-bearers with the right signature. | Incalculable |
| **Relic Transmitters** | Disc-shaped devices near the Vault zone. Emit a signal the Archive has partially decoded as a map of something that matches no known tunnel system. | The Archive pays more for these than anything else |
| **Relic Weapons** | Extremely rare. Objects that concentrate and discharge relic energy. The Saints confiscate every one they find. | Priceless, and dangerous to possess |
| **The Vault** | The largest known relic structure. Sealed chamber of indeterminate depth beneath the metro's lowest level. Entered once. Never re-entered. | The central mystery |

### The Danger
Prolonged exposure to relic energy causes measurable changes in mark-bearers — tracked in the game as **Corruption**. Not physical deformity: perceptual shift. Heightened sensitivity, increased mark activation, and in some cases, knowing the location of relic objects without having been near them. There is no clean return to zero.

## 5. The Wrist Mark

A sigil on the inner left wrist, approximately palm-sized. Faintly luminescent in low light — a violet pulse, regular as a heartbeat but not synchronised with the bearer's actual heartbeat. The lines are precise and impossibly fine, as if engraved from within. It does not hurt. It does not fade. It cannot be removed.

### What It Does
- Activates relic objects within approximately two meters
- Responds to relic technology — heating or pulsing when near active relics
- Opens certain relic locks (not all — each lock has its own bioelectric signature)
- Accumulates relic exposure (Corruption), amplifying range and sensitivity
- At high Corruption: displays fragments of what appears to be language
- At maximum Corruption: reportedly shows the bearer the location of the Vault, from inside

### How Factions Treat Mark-Bearers

| Faction | Response |
|---|---|
| Iron Saints | Register and monitor. High-trust Saints confiscate relics. Low-trust Saints arrest on sight. |
| Glass Court | Study. Mark-bearers are valuable research subjects. The Court pays well for voluntary access and coerces if given the opportunity. |
| Hollow Children | Cautious reverence. Metro folklore has built up around the marks. Some see bearers as protectors; others see them as cursed. |
| Red Choir | Sacred. The Choir believes mark-bearers are chosen. They want the player to join them. They are wrong about why, but right about the significance. |
| Last Archive | The only group with genuine documented knowledge about marks. They will share it — carefully, and only in exchange for trust. |

### Theories About the Mark's Origin
1. The Vault activated them remotely as part of whatever response the opening triggered.
2. The marks were always latent in certain individuals; the Blackout was the catalyst that expressed them.
3. The marks are the relic civilisation's method of selecting successors or messengers.
4. The marks are a warning system, and the Corruption accumulation is the countdown.

*None of these theories are confirmed in Chapters 1–4. By the end of the full game, one of them is clearly true — and it should not be the most comfortable one.*

## 6. Corruption

Corruption is not moral failure. It is the accumulation of relic energy exposure in a mark-bearer's system — a biological and perceptual change that cannot be reversed. It is the mark learning.

**Scale:** 0 to 100. Rises through relic contact, certain choices, and mark activation. Never decreases.

| Range | Label | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| 0–20 | Latent | Mark activates relics passively. No visible changes. |
| 21–40 | Aware | Player begins sensing relic locations without line of sight. Archive scholars notice and react differently. |
| 41–60 | Resonant | Mark activates at greater range. Relic locks previously closed may open. The Red Choir approaches with more urgency. |
| 61–80 | Saturated | Corruption choices bypass normal skill requirements. Some companions react with unease. Faction attitudes shift. |
| 81–100 | Consumed | The mark displays fragments of unknown language. The player receives information that could not be known by ordinary means. Hallucinations trigger. The Corruption ending becomes available. |

### Hallucinations (Corruption ≥ 70)
At high Corruption the game injects hallucination nodes mid-play — brief visions that interrupt the current scene and return the player to the real story when resolved. Three hallucination types exist:
- **The Mark Speaks** — A voice arrives without a mouth. It says the others lasted longer.
- **The Dead Speak** — People who have died in the player's presence appear in a tunnel. They are not angry. That is the worst part.
- **The Throne** — The rebuilt city, viewed from somewhere high. The player is sitting in it. Something is in their left hand that they do not want to look at.

### Important: Corruption Is Not Villainy
High-Corruption characters are not monsters. They are people who have been changed by something they didn't choose. Some of the most morally admirable choices in the game are only available at high Corruption — because only someone who has touched that depth of relic knowledge can see the path. Equally: low Corruption is not virtue. Staying "clean" means staying ignorant. The full picture of what happened requires knowing things that cost something.

## 7. Hunger and Scarcity

Hunger and thirst are not background numbers. They are the **moral context of every choice**. When the player has plenty, they can afford generosity. When they are empty, they face what they are actually willing to do.

### Survival Stats

| Stat | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hunger | Fullness. Drains slowly. At 0: morale drops. At 0 hunger + 0 morale: survival ending. |
| Thirst | Hydration. Drains faster than hunger. Contaminated water is a constant risk. At 0: health drops faster. |
| Health | Physical condition. Injuries reduce it. At 0: death ending. |
| Morale | Mental state. Does not kill directly, but opens dark options at very low values. |
| Corruption | Relic exposure. Never decreases. Unlocks relic-gated choices. Drives the hidden plot. |
| Trust | General survivor trust — community belief in the player. Affects faction attitudes collectively. |

### Food Distribution by Faction
- **Iron Saints:** Ration by compliance. Registrants are fed. Unregistered starve.
- **Hollow Children:** Share freely, but have the least.
- **Glass Court:** Stockpile and experiment on their own supplies.
- **Red Choir:** Consecrate provisions with ritual. Believers eat; doubters are offered.
- **Last Archive:** Trade information for provisions.

### Writing Guidance
Write scarcity as weight, not as a penalty. A player who chooses to share their last food with a stranger should feel that sacrifice — not just see a number change. If a scene involves eating or drinking, note the context of what the player's supplies look like. Survival stats should be reflected in narration, not abstracted into menu checks.

## 8. The Ruined City — All Zones

The city has no canonical name in the game. Characters call it "the City," "the Scar," or by specific district names. The name died with the people who used it daily.

### Surface Zones

#### The Scar District
The former city centre, closest to the Vault site below. Highest structural damage — buildings half-collapsed, streets opened by subsidence. Iron Saints maintain one patrol route. Relic density is highest here. **Feel:** Oppressive. Physically dangerous. The ruins here feel watched.

#### The Outer Ring
Former residential districts on the city periphery. Less damaged but largely depopulated. Wind comes through without obstruction. Vegetation has begun to reclaim the streets. **Feel:** Lonely. Vast. Occasional strange beauty.

#### The Checkpoint Corridor *(Chapter 1–2)*
A fortified strip connecting three Iron Saints surface positions. Razor wire, wand-equipped soldiers, identity processing. The official route between underground and surface. **Feel:** Bureaucratic violence. Everything is procedure. The procedure is designed to extract compliance.

#### The Glass Quarter *(Chapter 2, 5)*
Former scientific and administrative district. The Glass Court maintains several above-ground facilities powered by relic energy they somehow still have access to. **Feel:** Wrong kind of normal. Too orderly for what surrounds it.

#### District 4 — The Surface *(Chapter 2)*
Open ground above the metro network. Iron Saints patrol in force. Ash drifts through intersections that used to move with people. Three days of survival here test every resource. **Feel:** Exposed. Relentless. The sky is the wrong colour.

#### The Eastern Quarter *(Chapter 2, 6)*
Red Choir territory. A former concert hall converted to cathedral use. The Choir's broadcasts reach farther from here than anywhere else in the city. **Feel:** Feverish. Communal. Something is resonating in the walls.

---

### Underground Zones

#### Metro Line 9 — The Starting Zone *(Chapter 1)*
Sub-level 3 of the old Line 9 metro. Where the player begins. Emergency lighting, stripped resources, scattered survivor evidence. **Key locations:** The Ruined Station, Platform 9-B Vending Row, Service Corridor North, the Concourse Junction, Metro Security Room.

#### The Hollow Warrens *(Chapter 1, 3)*
Hollow Children territory. A network of converted maintenance tunnels, roughly habitable. Lanterns, salvaged furniture, communal sleeping areas. **Feel:** Crowded. Warm. Precarious. Community built from nothing.

#### The Saints Processing Hub *(Chapter 1, 4)*
Iron Saints underground administrative centre. Former metro management offices, converted to ID processing, supply control, and detention. **Feel:** Authority without legitimacy. Everything is documented. Documentation is control.

#### The Black Market Vein *(Chapter 1, 2)*
Informal trade zone in decommissioned tunnels. No faction officially controls it, which is why it exists. **Feel:** Clandestine vitality. Dangerous honesty. People who have nothing left to lose.

#### The Last Archive Entrance *(Chapter 1, 2, 7)*
A sealed section of pre-Blackout metro infrastructure. The outer hall is accessible; the inner sanctum requires invitation, a specific mark signature, or both. **Feel:** Old knowledge. Paper smell. Controlled desperation to preserve something.

#### The Vault Approaches *(Chapters 2, 6, 7)*
The tunnels leading to the Vault excavation site. Officially sealed by Iron Saints. Actually used by Archive researchers, Choir pilgrims, and the desperate. Heavy relic density. The mark reacts constantly here. **Feel:** Wrong. Something happened here and is still happening. The air feels thicker.

#### The Hollow Sanctum — Sublevel 7 *(Chapter 3)*
The political and communal heart of the Hollow Children's deeper warrens. Further from the surface than Line 9. Kira's meeting spaces, the Builder Quarter, the Walker staging tunnels, and the Archive Observation Post all branch from here. **Feel:** Underground civilisation. Fragile. Detailed. Something is wrong with the changed child in the back room.

#### The Flooded Sublevel 9 — The Record Room *(Chapter 3)*
A pre-Blackout research basement that flooded before the Blackout but retained its documentation on sealed shelving. Accessible only to those who know the tunnel path. Contains pre-collapse records proving the city was built over something intentional. **Feel:** Cold water. Submerged history. Paper that should not have survived.

#### Iron Saints Northern Fortress *(Chapter 4)*
The Iron Saints' primary underground command post. Inquisitor's Records Hall, the Sinner Registry, training yards, the confession hall. **Feel:** Institutional. Disciplined. The specific violence of a bureaucracy with a mandate.

#### The Checkpoint Grid — Sector 4 *(Chapter 4)*
The Saints' checkpoint network at its densest — the player must navigate this as either a registered citizen or a covert operative. **Feel:** Surveillance. Every movement documented. The wand operators watch everything.

#### Sera's Private Office *(Chapter 4)*
Inquisitor Sera's records room within the fortress. She knew about the Archive before the Blackout and deliberately concealed it from the order's founder. The documentation of this is somewhere in this room. **Feel:** Quiet authority. Everything here is a choice about what to know and what to say.

## 9. Major Factions

### The Iron Saints
*"Order is survival."*

**Theme colour:** `#8e3a2a` — deep rust-red
**Shorthand:** Saints / Saint patrols

#### Beliefs
Without structure, the remaining eighty thousand survivors will consume each other within a year. The Iron Saints believe — genuinely, most of them — that their mandate is the preservation of what remains of civilisation. They are not wrong that order matters. They are wrong about who gets to define it, and at what cost.

The Saints have a deep institutional fear of relic technology after the Blackout. Their solution is elimination: confiscate every relic object, register every mark-bearer, and systematically reduce the number of variables they cannot predict.

#### Leader: Commander Aldric Voss
**Age:** 58. Former city military officer who survived the Blackout underground in a Saints bunker. Lost his family in the first week. Has not spoken of them since. Rebuilt the Saints from a communication-severed unit into a functioning authority through sheer will and documented procedure. Not cruel by disposition — cruel by policy. The distinction has stopped mattering.

**Secret:** Voss has a mark-bearing informant embedded in every faction except the Choir. He knows more about the marks than he admits. He also knows the Vault was deliberately opened, and he knows who authorised it.

#### Inquisitor: Sera *(Chapter 4 antagonist)*
**Age:** 41. The Saints' senior Inquisitor, stationed at the Northern Fortress. She knew about the Archive's existence before the Blackout and deliberately concealed it from the order's founder. This secret is her leverage — and her liability. Cain's full betrayal or redemption arc plays out through her.

Sera is not a true believer. She is a pragmatist who understood early that the Saints were the vehicle most likely to survive the Blackout's aftermath. She is more dangerous than Voss for precisely this reason: she has no ideology to compromise with.

#### Strengths
- Largest organised force — approximately 3,000 active members
- Control of the most reliable food distribution network (for registrants)
- The only functional medical infrastructure above baseline
- The wands — ability to detect relics and marks at checkpoints
- Documentation — they know more about who is alive and where than anyone

#### Weaknesses
- Institutional fear of relic technology limits their understanding of it
- Internal fractures — some Saints believe the mandate has gone too far
- Legitimacy is self-declared since the Blackout severed command structure
- Mark-bearer registration is incomplete — they cannot find what they are not told about

#### Possible Alliance Route
The player aligns with the Saints by embracing order — registering, complying, demonstrating that a mark-bearer can be controlled. Aldric Voss respects demonstrated consistency above all things. At maximum trust, the player learns: Voss knows the Vault was deliberately opened, and he knows who ordered it.

#### Possible Ending
**FACTION ENDING:** The player uses their mark and their Saints relationship to broker a formal agreement — Saints authority in exchange for protected status for mark-bearers and Archive independence. Voss agrees, conditionally. The city gets order. Mark-bearers get the right to exist. Nobody gets the truth about the Vault. A compromise ending. Not heroic. Not comfortable. Sustainable.

### The Glass Court
*"Knowledge is survival."*

**Theme colour:** `#2a5a6a` — deep teal
**Shorthand:** the Court / Court researchers

#### Beliefs
The right application of intelligence can solve any resource problem — and they were largely correct until the Vault. Five years later, they still believe it. Their internal culture is rational, hierarchical, and quietly monstrous. They do not think of themselves as doing harm. They think of every sacrifice as a necessary data point. The fact that the data points are people is something they have learned not to look at directly.

#### Leader: Director Seraphine Kael
**Age:** 44. A physicist who was present at the Glass Court's first successful relic energy extraction. Not present at the Vault opening — she was in a different facility. She watched the Blackout happen on instruments that went dead in sequence. She has been trying to understand it ever since.

**Secret:** Kael ordered the Vault opening. She did not understand what would happen. She has never admitted this and has covered every trace. The guilt has made her more ruthless, not less. She also knew about the marks before the Blackout — the Glass Court had documentation suggesting that certain bioelectric signatures responded to relic technology. She shared this with Cain, who has been sitting on it for five years.

#### Strengths
- Functional laboratory equipment powered by a surviving relic cache
- Medical knowledge — the best doctors in the city work for the Court
- The only group systematically studying mark behaviour and relic interaction
- Control of three above-ground facilities the Saints have not entered (yet)
- Information — they have documented the city continuously since before the Blackout

#### Weaknesses
- Their relic cache powering their facilities is finite
- Internal ethical fractures — some researchers are breaking from Kael's methods
- The Saints tolerate them but do not trust them — one incident from conflict
- They need mark-bearers for research and cannot fully recruit; coercion creates enemies
- Kael's secret is a structural weakness — exposure would collapse the Court's legitimacy

#### Possible Alliance Route
The player aligns with the Glass Court by committing to the research mandate — sharing mark activations, providing relic objects, gaining Kael's trust enough to learn the truth about the Vault opening. The alliance ending requires the player to choose whether to expose her or protect her secret for the greater good of the Court's continuing research.

#### Possible Ending
**FACTION ENDING:** The player, holding information about Kael's role in the Vault opening, becomes the Court's de facto moral compass. Protect Kael and stabilise the Court — the research continues. Expose her — the Court fractures but the information becomes public. Either way, the player has changed what the Court is.

### The Hollow Children
*"Survival is communal or it is nothing."*

**Theme colour:** `#5a7a3a` — muted green
**Shorthand:** the Hollow / Hollow kids / the Warrens

#### Beliefs
The Hollow Children formed in the metro tunnels during the first winter — people who had nothing pooling what little they had. Their ethics are simple and extremely difficult: share what you can, protect who you can, ask nothing that you wouldn't give yourself. They do not have a political ideology. They have a practice. That practice is fragile and beautiful.

#### Leader: Kira
**Age:** 67. A former metro maintenance supervisor who guided survivors through the tunnel system by memory on the night of the Blackout. Has lived in the Warrens since. Has never been to the surface since the Blackout. Leads by example, endurance, and an absolute refusal to eat before the children eat.

In Chapter 3, Kira's leadership comes under pressure from two internal factions — the **Builders** (fortify and survive) and the **Walkers** (reach the Archive). Navigating this political split is the central conflict of that chapter.

**Secret:** Kira has a mark. She has had it since the Blackout. She has told no one. She has used it twice, both times to find water in tunnels no one else knew about. She will reveal this only to a player with maximum trust, and only when she is certain they will not use it against her community.

#### Strengths
- Largest civilian population — approximately 15,000 people in the Warrens
- Genuine community loyalty — intelligence about movements comes freely here
- Deep tunnel knowledge — Hollow guides can move players through the metro unseen
- The moral centre of the city — helping the Hollow has cascading goodwill effects
- Access to salvaged resources no faction has catalogued because they are not organised

#### Weaknesses
- No military capacity to speak of — Hollow fighters exist but are few and lightly armed
- Completely dependent on continuing to receive some resources from above
- Any faction that cuts their supply has immediate leverage
- Openness makes them vulnerable to infiltration (Saints informants are in the Warrens)
- The lack of clear succession after Kira is a structural risk

#### Possible Alliance Route
The player becomes a champion of the Warrens — using their mark and faction connections to secure resources and protection. Maximum trust unlocks Kira revealing her mark, and eventually brokering a formal protected status for the Warrens that every other faction must acknowledge.

#### Possible Ending
**COMPANION/FACTION ENDING:** The player, using every connection they have built, secures formal recognition of the Warrens as an autonomous community zone — exempt from Saints processing, supplied by Court medical resources, protected by an Archive-brokered agreement. Kira survives to see it. She does not thank the player directly. She just keeps eating after the children eat. That is enough.

### The Red Choir
*"The Blackout was not a disaster. It was a gift."*

**Theme colour:** `#7a1a3a` — deep crimson
**Shorthand:** the Choir / Choir broadcasts

#### Beliefs
The Red Choir believes the Vault opened because it was time to open it. They believe the marks are chosen. They believe the marks are being called. They are not entirely wrong — the pattern they are reacting to is real. Their interpretation of why is catastrophically, dangerously wrong.

#### Leader: The Voice
**Age:** Unknown. Broadcasts every third day from a location that has never been found despite serious effort. Whether the Voice is one person or a rotating position is unknown outside the inner Choir. What is known: the Voice has a mark. Their Corruption is extremely high. They see things that have not happened yet. They are not a liar — their visions are genuine. Their interpretation of those visions is the danger.

**Secret:** The Voice is not alone in the broadcast location. They are with three other mark-bearers whose Corruption is near or at maximum. They are not worshipping the Vault. They are communicating with it.

#### Strengths
- The only faction that actively recruits mark-bearers, offering genuine community
- Broadcast reach — the Voice can communicate with most of the metro network
- Deep understanding of relic geography — they have mapped more of the underground than the Archive
- Absolute member loyalty — no Saints informants have successfully penetrated the inner Choir
- Access to the Vault approaches — they have routes no other faction has found

#### Weaknesses
- Perceived as dangerous or insane by the majority of survivors
- No conventional resource base — dependent on member provision and relic trade
- Their goal (Vault reopening) is opposed by every other faction
- High Corruption among leadership means decision-making is increasingly non-standard
- The Voice's genuine visions are not clearly distinguishable from delusion, even from inside

#### Possible Alliance Route
The player aligns with the Choir by following the Voice deep into the Vault approaches and surviving what they find there — which confirms some of what the Choir believes and invalidates other parts. The alliance ending does not involve completing the Choir's original mission. It involves the player helping the Voice understand what they are actually hearing from the Vault, which is not what they thought.

#### Possible Ending
**SECRET ENDING:** The player, working with the Voice, discovers the actual nature of what is in the Vault — not a god, not a weapon, not a warning, but a message. The ending reveals the message — not in words, but in the player character's understanding. The game does not tell the player what they now know. They carry it out into the world. That is the end.

### The Last Archive
*"Knowledge must be preserved, not wielded."*

**Theme colour:** `#6a5a2a` — aged amber
**Shorthand:** the Archive / Archive scholars

#### Beliefs
The Last Archive was founded to document the relic findings and ensure that information was not lost if the political situation changed — which it did, dramatically. They were right to preserve it. They were wrong to think preservation was enough.

The Archive's failure is the failure of the scholar who watches the fire from a safe distance taking careful notes. They have the most knowledge. They have used it least effectively. They know more about the marks than anyone, and they are still waiting for the right conditions to act.

#### Head Archivist: Velna
**Age:** 54 at the time of her disappearance. A linguist and one of the original Archive team forty years ago. The only member of that team who successfully partially decoded three relic languages. She knows what the Vault transmitters are broadcasting. She entered the secondary Archive access point during the events of the game and left a message: *"It's larger than I expected. I'm going further."* She has not returned.

**Secret:** Velna partially decoded the Vault transmitter signal. It is not a warning. It is not an invitation. It is a question. She did not know the answer. She went into the Archive to find it.

*Note: With Velna missing, the Archive's leadership is in crisis. The player encounters her message in the secret ending route — following the coordinates she left to the secondary entrance, finding her words on the wall just inside.*

She has no mark. She was the only major faction leader without one.

#### Strengths
- The most accurate and complete knowledge of relic technology and mark behaviour
- Sole holders of the partial Vault transmitter translation
- Extensive pre-Blackout documentation — they know what the city was
- The only faction operating on genuine diplomatic neutrality (all factions need what they know)
- Access to the inner door — the most relic-dense space outside the Vault itself

#### Weaknesses
- Smallest faction — perhaps 200 active scholars, and their head archivist is gone
- No military capacity, dependent entirely on diplomatic neutrality
- The tendency to know things and not act on them is both their strength and their central flaw
- Everyone wants access to the inner door and the Archive has no good way to share it safely
- Velna's incomplete translation is the most dangerous document in the city

#### Possible Alliance Route
The player becomes the Archive's agent in the field — doing what scholars cannot: moving through faction lines, accessing relic sites, and returning with data. Maximum trust unlocks the partial translation and eventually Velna's coordinates — the closest thing to a master key for the game's mysteries.

#### Possible Ending
**SECRET ENDING:** The player, with maximum Archive trust and high Corruption, enters the secondary door using Velna's coordinates and receives the full translation — not as language but as direct understanding transferred through the mark. The Vault's question is answered. Not by fighting, not by choosing, but by witnessing. The Archive begins building something for the first time in five years.

## 10. Major Companions

### Mira
**Role:** Former Last Archive extraction specialist, working as a street medic. The player's first reliable ally.
**Age:** 32

**Appearance:** Medium height, practical. Hair kept very short — a maintenance habit that stuck. Always carries a salvaged tool kit. Her hands are scarred from the first winter.

**True Backstory:** Mira was not just a metro maintenance engineer. Before the Blackout, she was a Last Archive **extraction specialist** — her job was to get texts out of places that were about to stop existing. She got very good at moving fast and not asking what she was carrying. She knew about the marks three years before the Blackout. She knew what they were being designed for. She didn't tell anyone who wasn't already in the room.

After the Blackout she reinvented herself as a street medic — deliberately. It was the role least likely to attract Archive or faction attention. She has been watching for a mark-bearer she could trust. The player is the first.

She does not have a mark. She has thought carefully about what it would mean if she did.

**Personality:** Honest to the point of abruptness. Does not perform comfort — gives it directly or not at all. Has a very specific sense of what is fair and will not compromise on it in ways that destroy her. Reads the player accurately and quickly. Does not mention it.

**What She Brings:**
- Tunnel access — routes that are not on any map
- Mechanical knowledge — can identify relic objects by function without touching them
- Honest assessment — she will tell the player when they are wrong, clearly
- Community connection — the Hollow Children trust her unconditionally

**Trust Arc:** Trust builds through honesty, protecting people at personal cost, not lying about what the mark is doing, and listening when she talks about the first week (she only does this once — it matters enormously).

At Trust 5: She reveals she was Last Archive, not just maintenance.
At Trust 7: She tells the player she found the bodies of the Vault extraction team — not in the Vault, in the tunnels below it, arranged, all facing the same direction.
At Trust 10: The companion ending. She asks the player one question.

**Companion Interlude Events:**
1. *What She Carried Out* — She tells the player she was Last Archive. "I am telling you now."
2. *The Mark Is Changing You* — Corruption warning: "I've seen three people carry marks like yours. Two of them stopped being recognisable as themselves."
3. *The Space Between Us* — She confronts the player about their direction: "I need to know if that's who you're becoming or if it's who you've always been."
4. *If It Comes to It* — She tells the player she has already decided about the Archive door. "I want you to know I have already made the decision."

**Sacrifice Condition:** Mira can sacrifice herself to save the Hollow Children's Warren from a Saints purge — but only if the player failed to broker a Saints deal AND Mira's trust is high enough that she makes the choice independently. Her death is not shown on screen. The player arrives after. The Hollow Children remember her name.

### Cain
**Role:** Former Black Market courier. The player's most useful and least trustworthy ally.
**Age:** 27

**Appearance:** Lean, quick. Always wearing something with too many pockets. Eyes that calculate distance before other things. A quality of stillness that suggests the movement that follows will be fast.

**Backstory:** Cain was running routes through the metro black market before the Blackout — good at it, well-connected, entirely amoral in the way the market rewarded. He has a mark. He has known since the morning after the Blackout. He has told no one — not because he fears it, but because information is currency and he hasn't found the right buyer yet.

**The Secret He's Sitting On:** The Glass Court knew about the marks before the Blackout. Director Kael told him. They had documentation. He has been sitting on this for five years. He will tell the player at maximum trust — and only the player.

**Personality:** Charming in the way of someone who learned charm as a tool. Genuinely funny. Occasionally genuinely kind, at moments that surprise him as much as everyone else. Honest about being dishonest, which is a form of honesty he values highly. Will not lie to the player about his own nature. Will lie to everyone else without hesitation.

**What He Brings:**
- Market access — he knows every trader, route, and price
- Faction intelligence — he runs between all factions without full allegiance to any
- Mark knowledge — his own experience gives the player the most honest peer perspective available
- A specific Saints contact who is corruptible — useful at the right moment

**Trust Arc:**
At Trust 3: He acknowledges the player's mark without exploitation — this is rare.
At Trust 7: He reveals which faction he has been reporting to. Not the Saints. The Glass Court. They knew.
At Trust 10: The companion ending — two people who have been honest with each other about the exact nature of their dishonesty. Quiet. Specific.

**Companion Interlude Events:**
1. *The Ledger* — He says one word: "Capable." With Cain, this is a speech.
2. *The Inventory* — He has already counted everything. The numbers are bad. "I have been assuming you understand."
3. *The Cost of Waiting* — "Three times you had a clear path and you waited." He counts. He always counts.
4. *The Other Offer* — "The Iron Saints sent a message three days ago. The offer expires in forty-eight hours. I have not responded. I am telling you that too."

**Betrayal Condition:** Cain betrays if his trust is below 3 AND the player has a specific flag AND registers with the Saints at the Processing Hub. He turns in the registration information. It's not personal. He says this in the betrayal scene. He means it, and it doesn't help.

## 11. Major Villains and Antagonists

> No character in this game is a villain by nature. Every antagonist believes they are doing what must be done. The horror is not that they are wrong about the facts. Sometimes they are not. The horror is what they are willing to do with facts that are correct.

### Commander Aldric Voss (Iron Saints)
Voss is right that the city needs structure to survive. He is right that mark-bearers represent an unpredictable variable. He is wrong that controlling unpredictable variables is sufficient, and wrong about the scale of what is underneath him. His final confrontation is a conversation, not a fight. He already knows the player cannot be stopped. He wants to know if they are worth what they are about to do.

### Inquisitor Sera (Iron Saints)
Sera is the Saints' chief Inquisitor and the most dangerous character in Chapter 4. Unlike Voss, she has no ideological investment in the Saints' mandate — she is a pragmatist who chose the winning vehicle early and has been steering it ever since. She knew about the Archive before the Blackout and concealed it from the founder. She is not protecting anyone with this secret. She is protecting her leverage.

Her arc resolves in one of three ways: the player exposes her and dismantles her authority, the player uses her secret as leverage to negotiate from inside, or Cain's betrayal/redemption arc intersects with her in a way that changes both of them. She cannot be defeated in the conventional sense. She can be outmanoeuvred.

### Director Seraphine Kael (Glass Court)
Kael opened the Vault. She caused the Blackout. She has spent five years trying to fix it using the same framework of thinking that caused it. She genuinely believes more knowledge will produce a solution. The only path that fully resolves her arc is the one where she understands what she opened. Not what it did. What it is.

### The Voice (Red Choir)
The Voice is experiencing genuine visions. Their interpretation of those visions is the danger — not because they are lying, but because they are translating something they can feel but not fully comprehend. Their final scene is the most ambiguous in the game. A player at maximum Corruption will understand something about the Voice's visions that the Voice does not: they are not instructions. They are distress signals. The Voice has been trying to help something that is asking to be helped. Their method is wrong. Their instinct is not.

## 12. Important Mysteries

### What is in the Vault?
**Partial answers:** The relic civilisation built the Vault as a communication point. Something is still active inside it. The extraction team that opened it was not harmed — they were responded to.
**Full answer:** *NOT REVEALED IN CHAPTERS 1–4. Revealed in Chapter 7.*
**Writing note:** Do not answer this. Plant evidence. Let each faction's interpretation feel plausible.

### Who built the relic civilisation?
**Partial answers:** Their architecture predates human civilisation by an unknown period. Their materials are not Earth-origin. The marks appear to be their interface technology.
**Full answer:** *DELIBERATELY AMBIGUOUS. The game does not confirm or deny external origin.*
**Writing note:** Never say alien. Never say divine. Let the player sit with not knowing.

### Why these specific mark-bearers?
**Full answer:** The marks identify people with a specific bioelectric signature that the relic technology uses as a key. The selection criteria is mechanical, not chosen. There is nothing special about the people — there is something specific about the signal they produce.
**Writing note:** This is revealed at maximum Archive trust. It is both disappointing and liberating. The player was not chosen. They are useful. That distinction matters.

### What caused the Blackout?
**Full answer:** The Vault responded to being opened by neutralising all energy systems in its vicinity — a defensive measure miscalibrated for the scale of human development. It was not trying to kill the city. It was trying to stop something. It did not know the city was there.
**Writing note:** This revelation is the single most important story beat. The Blackout was not malice. That is worse than malice in some ways.

### What does the Vault transmitter signal mean?
**Full answer:** It is a question: *"Is anyone still there?"* The relic civilisation has been broadcasting through the Vault transmitters, waiting for something with the right bioelectric signature to respond. The marks are the response mechanism. Nobody responded until now.
**Writing note:** This is the emotional core of the entire game. Write every ending with this as the background.

### Where is Velna?
**Partial answer:** She left coordinates to a secondary archive entrance and a message: *"It's larger than I expected. I'm going further."*
**Full answer:** Revealed only in the secret ending route. Follow the coordinates. Find what she found.
**Writing note:** Do not close this mystery before Chapter 7. The player should find it themselves.

## 14. Chapter Roadmap

### Prologue: The Blackout *(Planned)*
Three years before the main story. A series of vignettes: the night the city-wide blackout occurred. Each playable background class gets its own scene — the moment they were underground, and the moment the mark appeared. The prologue establishes tone, key factions, and the personal reason each background character was underground on that night. No resources drain. No companion encounters. Always advances to Chapter 1.

**Flags introduced:** `prologue_complete`, `background_vignette_played`, `knows_blackout_origin`

The player wakes in the metro. The mark is new. Survival is immediate. The factions are establishing themselves around the player. The companions are encountered. The central mystery is presented but not named.

The chapter ends returning underground.

### Chapter 5: The Glass Court *(Planned)*
The Glass Court presents itself as a secular, scientific faction. The truth: they were founded by pre-collapse researchers who knew the Archive existed and have been trying to access it since the Blackout. They have seventeen marked individuals in containment. None of their extractions have survived intact.

---

### Chapter 6: The Red Choir *(Planned)*
The Red Choir recruits through sound — frequencies broadcast through the metro's old speaker systems that produce a genuine physiological response in people who carry the mark. ### Chapter 7: The Last Archive *(Planned)*
The player enters the Last Archive for the first time — a vast underground structure predating every faction, every city, every record. ---

### Chapter 8: Hunger War *(Planned)*
Three months after Chapter 7. A food crisis has accelerated every faction conflict. The Iron Saints are seizing supply caches. The Glass Court is rationing through algorithmic triage. The Hollow Children are starving. The Red Choir is offering something that suppresses hunger and is not food.

### Chapter 9: The Mark Awakens *(Planned)*
The Corruption system reaches its consequence point: high Corruption opens unique paths but begins permanently altering the player's physiology. ## 15. Ending Philosophy

> No ending is the good ending. No ending is the bad ending. Every ending is the ending that player became.

### Rules
- Every ending should feel earned by the player's specific choices, not by a final decision alone.
- Death endings are not failures — they are a world in which someone with that mark, those stats, those allies, in that moment, did not make it. That is allowed.
- The "best" ending is whichever one the player would make again. Not the one that maximises survival.
- Companion endings are only available if the companion is alive. Death is permanent. Do not make this avoidable.
- Corruption endings should be written without judgment. A player at maximum Corruption is not a bad player. They are a changed one.

### Ending Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Death** | Violent or survival failure. Written with gravity, not punishment. The last thing the player sees should be worth seeing. |
| **Betrayal** | Someone the player trusted turned. The ending should make clear why, without excusing it. |
| **Sacrifice** | The player gives something that cannot come back — including, potentially, themselves — to protect something that matters. |
| **Tyrant** | The player wins in the way that requires becoming what they feared. Not evil. Just wrong, in the specific way available to people who are not wrong about everything. |
| **Savior** | The player finds a way through that most people would not have found. Rare. Not heroic in tone — just true. |
| **Companion** | Two people, at the end of everything, choosing to stay with each other. Quiet. Specific. Earned. |
| **Faction** | The player's chosen faction wins something real. The city changes. Not perfectly. It never does. |
| **Secret** | The player carries something out that no one else has. Not power. Knowledge. The responsibility is theirs now. |
| **Corruption** | The mark consumes. This is not death. It is transformation. Write it with the same care as the Savior ending. |
| **Wanderer** | The player walks away from the question. Not answering is an answer. Give them the road. |

## 16. Tone Guide

### Atmosphere
The game is dark — not darkness for spectacle, but the specific weight of a world where things are genuinely hard and people are genuinely trying. The darkness has texture. It has specific smells (rust, damp concrete, woodsmoke). It has sounds (the metro's structural groan, the distant sound of generators, the silence that follows them). It has temperature. Write with physical precision.

### Violence
Violence in this game is not cinematic. It is expensive and it leaves damage that persists. Write the cost first. The tactical result second. Nobody wins a fight without paying something.

### Hope
The game is bleak. It is also, underneath everything, about the specific kind of hope that exists when you are in a situation that does not promise resolution. Not optimism. Not heroism. The simple fact of continuing. People help each other here. Not always. Not reliably. But when they do, write it with the same weight as the violence. A stranger sharing clean water in the metro is as consequential as a gunshot. Often more.

### World-Building
Do not explain the world. Let the world be apparent. A player should understand the faction landscape from how people talk about it, not from exposition. Trust the player to notice. Trust the player to ask. Give them things to ask about.

## 17. Writing Rules

### Voice
- Second person present tense. Always. "You open your eyes." Not "The character opened their eyes."
- Do not describe the player's emotions. Describe what they observe. The player supplies the feeling.
- Narration is the camera. What it shows matters. What it does not show matters more.
- Dialogue is specific. People in this world talk like people who do not have time for subtext — and then reveal that everything they said was subtext.
- Use the location as a character. The metro pushes back. So does the surface.

### Structure
- Every node needs one thing the player will remember — one image, one line, one physical detail.
- End narration on an edge. The next choice should feel like the thing that relieves or extends the tension, not like a prompt.
- If the node is longer than three paragraphs, split it. Pacing in text is white space.
- Dialogue nodes should feel like interruptions to narration, not the reverse.
- Ending nodes should slow down. The last thing the player reads before the ending screen should earn the screen.

### Things to Avoid
- **Exposition dumps.** No character explains the world to other characters who already live in it.
- **Morality signposting.** Do not use words like "cruel," "kind," or "evil" about choices. Show what the choice costs.
- **Cheap redemption.** If someone is redeemed, it should hurt getting there.
- **Chosen one framing.** The marks are not destiny. They are a coincidence that the player must make meaning of.
- **Safe choices.** Every choice should be a real choice — not a right answer with a wrong answer attached.
- **Violence as punctuation.** Every death should matter. Every injury should persist.

## 18. Choice Design Rules

### The Principle
Every choice is a question about who the player is, asked through the mechanics of what the player can do. "Step forward. If they want a fight, give them one." is not the same as "Fight." The first is a character. The second is a menu option.

### Rules
- Write choices as the first-person thought or action, not as a menu description.
- All choices should feel like real decisions. No obvious right answers. The player should sometimes regret the right one.
- Always include at least one choice with no stat or flag requirements — a path for everyone.
- Stat-gated choices should be aspirational, not frustrating. A locked choice should make the player want to have that stat.
- Effects should be proportional. A small kindness gives small trust. A meaningful sacrifice gives meaningful change.
- Choices that betray companions or factions should not be labelled as such. The player makes the choice, then understands what they did.
- Never write a choice that is purely positive with no cost, or purely negative with no reason.

### Dice Design
- Dice checks are for moments where chance is narratively appropriate — not for every challenge.
- The failure path should still be interesting, not just a penalty.
- Critical success should feel like luck that the player didn't earn. Critical failure should feel like the world reminding them it's still the world.
- Luck rerolls are precious. Write the moment the player would use one as the kind of moment it would feel most meaningful in.
- Valid dice stats: `strength`, `intelligence`, `agility`, `charisma`, `luck`. Never `corruption` or `trust`.

---

## 19. Faction Design Rules

### The Principle
Every faction is right about something. Every faction is wrong about something. Faction conflict is not good vs. evil — it is incomplete information + real constraints + genuine fear + power dynamics. The player navigates that.

### Rules
- Faction members are individuals. Not every Saints soldier is Voss. Not every Choir member is the Voice.
- Faction reputation effects should be cumulative and visible — players should feel their choices building toward something.
- No faction should be universally correct. No faction should be a straw man.
- Faction endings are legitimate. Aligning with any faction should produce a meaningful outcome.
- Inter-faction dynamics shift based on player actions. A player who makes Saints-Hollow peace possible changes how every subsequent Saints interaction feels.

### Reputation Design
- Faction rep ranges from -100 to +100. -100 is armed conflict. +100 is full alliance.
- Most players will end somewhere in the middle range with most factions.
- Choices that gain rep with one faction should often cost with another.
- Rep gates should be readable — the player should understand why a choice requires a specific threshold before they need it.
- Maximum rep with a faction should reveal their flaw as clearly as their strength.

---

## 20. Companion Relationship Rules

### The Principle
Companion trust is earned by treating companions as people, not as resources. High trust is not loyalty purchased — it is a relationship built.

### Trust Scale (0–10)

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | The companion is present but wary |
| 3 | Starting trust for a chosen companion |
| 5 | Enough trust to share honest opinions and ask for help |
| 7 | Enough trust to share something the companion has not told anyone else |
| 9 | Enough trust for the companion to make an independent sacrifice |
| 10 | The companion ending is available |

**Typical deltas:**
- Small moment of honesty or care: **+1**
- Meaningful choice that respects the companion's stated values: **+2**
- Significant sacrifice or risk taken for the companion: **+3**
- Lie discovered / betrayal / using companion as a resource: **−1 to −3**

### Companion Interlude Events
Companions fire interlude events at key trust thresholds. These are brief scenes injected between story beats — not reachable through normal navigation. All interlude choices use `nextNode: '__return__'` to resume the real story. Flags set during interludes persist. Each event fires only once and sets a `_seen` flag.

Mira's four events trigger at: low corruption warning / first honest conversation / player direction challenged / sacrifice offer.
Cain's four events trigger at: first sign of respect / resource crisis / hesitation pattern / the other offer revelation.

### Writing Companions
- Companions have opinions. Write them. They do not always agree with the player's choice.
- A companion's response to a choice should reflect their personality specifically, not a generic approval signal.
- Companion dialogue is sparse — they do not speak in every scene. When they do, it matters.
- Companions can be wrong. Mira's assessment of a situation is not always correct. Cain's instincts sometimes fail him.
- Never write a companion as fully dependent on the player. They had lives before the player arrived. Those lives matter to them.
- Companion deaths are permanent. No retroactive saves. No grief shortcut.
